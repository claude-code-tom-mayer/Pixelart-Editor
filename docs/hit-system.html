Overview

A hit is a shape, not a scan

The G_HitServer autoload reads the one shared G_ChunkingServer autoload, follows its id lifecycle through its signals, and keeps only the hit side of every entity — the module references, the bands, the group masks and the radius profiles. A hit never walks the entity list: it turns its shape into an axis aligned box, asks the chunk grid which chunks that box touches, drops every chunk holding no opponent, and only then tests geometry.

That narrowing is exact, not approximate. An entity is registered in every chunk its radius touches, so any entity whose silhouette reaches into the box is already sitting in one of the chunks being walked. Nothing that a full scan would find can slip through.

Each shape comes twice. The plain version collects whatever it finds and is the cheap choice for a simple blast. The _ordered version sorts the targets along an axis that suits the shape, which is what makes a hit limit mean "the nearest three" and lets a stop group end the hit at the first thing that blocks it.

Filtering

Two group spaces that never mix

The chunking groups and the hit groups answer different questions and live in different bitmasks. Keeping them apart is deliberate: one is about what an entity is, the other about what can physically connect with it.

MaskWidth · ownerAnswers
_entityGroupsint64 · ChunkingWhat the entity is — ground, air, armored. Aggregated per chunk, column and map

Lives in G_ChunkingServer. Only targeting reads it — a hit never looks at it. Example: GROUND = 0b01, AIR = 0b10; a bat is 0b10.

_entityTargetedGroupsint64 · TargetingWhat it is allowed to go after

Lives in G_TargetingServer, same bit meanings as _entityGroups. An anti-air tower with 0b10 only ever picks bats as targets. Whether its shot then harms the bat is decided by the three masks below.

_entityHitGroupsint64 · HitWhat its hits are made of — projectile, melee, magic

The damage channels every hit of this entity carries. Hit, hurt and stop groups share one bit space — they are ANDed with each other — so define the channels once:

const CH_PROJECTILE: int = 1 << 0 # 0b001 const CH_MELEE: int = 1 << 1 # 0b010 const CH_SHIELD: int = 1 << 2 # 0b100 — never carried by an attack, marks a blocker

An archer's hits carry CH_PROJECTILE (0b001). A knight's carry CH_MELEE. A flaming sword could carry both.

_entityHurtGroupsint64 · HitWhat can physically harm it — a ghost simply has no projectile bit

A hit connects when emitter hit groups & target hurt groups != 0. Also what a stop group is matched against, so it carries "I am a blocker" bits like CH_SHIELD too.

TargethurtGroupsArrow 0b001 & hurtResult
goblin0b0110b001hit
shieldbearer0b1110b001hit
ghost0b0100b000untouched — the arrow passes through

An entity whose hurt groups are 0 can never be hit — including every entity registered without a profile.

_entityStopGroupsint64 · HitWhich hurt groups end its hit on the target that matches

Checked only by the _ordered hits, on targets that were already hit: once emitter stop groups & target hurt groups != 0, that target still takes the hit, and everything after it is spared. An archer with stopGroups = CH_SHIELD fires a piercing arrow:

A target that the hit does not connect with (the ghost) cannot stop it either — stop groups only look at targets that were hit.

## A hit connects when the two hit space masks overlap. emitter._entityHitGroups & target._entityHurtGroups != 0 ## An ordered hit ends on the target that blocks it. emitter._entityStopGroups & target._entityHurtGroups != 0

A shield that stops projectiles is a stop group on the attack, matched against the shield's hurt groups. A ghost immune to arrows simply lacks the projectile bit in its hurt groups — no special case anywhere in the code.

Resolution

From shape to dispatch

Geometry

The third axis and the radius profile

An entity's position is a Vector2 with no height in it. Height lives in the y band: (bottom, top), the vertical slice the entity occupies. A hit carries its own band, and the two must overlap before anything else is considered — a low sweep passes under a flying unit without a special case.

By default an entity is a cylinder: the same radius at every height. Give it a radius curve and it becomes a silhouette — narrow at the feet, wide at the shoulders, narrow again at the head.

## One sample per candidate, never a scan for a maximum. l_sampleHeight = clamp(middle of the hit band, band.bottom, band.top) l_percent = (l_sampleHeight - band.bottom) / band height * 100 l_radius = entity radius * curve.sample_baked(l_percent) / 100

The curve must be authored with domain 0 to 100 and value range 0 to 100. A curve left on Godot's default 0 to 1 domain clamps to its maximum and silently behaves like a cylinder.

Shapes

Three shapes, three orderings

ShapeDefined byOrdered by
circleorigin, radiusDistance from the origin, nearest first
directional rectorigin at the back edge, direction, length, widthThe chosen RECT_EDGE: BACK, FRONT, LEFT or RIGHT
cake sliceorigin at the tip, direction, radius, full angleSweep angle, right edge to left; p_isReversed flips it

The unordered variants do not evaluate stop groups. Without an order, "stops at the first blocker" would mean an arbitrary blocker, so the plain functions simply collect up to the hit limit. Use the ordered variant whenever the emitter has stop groups.

How to use

Arming an entity, then hitting with it

An entity takes its id from the chunk index, then hands the hit server that id, its module manager and an R_HitProfile. An entity that never registers here can never be hit. A recycled id always starts clean — the hit server clears the slot when the index emits s_entityReleased, so an entity can never inherit the groups or the profile of whoever held its id before. The setters exist for changes during a round.

## Hit channels — one bit space shared by hit, hurt and stop groups. const CH_PROJECTILE: int = 1 << 0 const CH_MELEE: int = 1 << 1 const CH_SHIELD: int = 1 << 2 ## Spawns one archer and arms it. ## @param p_position Where it spawns ## @return Its id on every server func spawn_archer(p_position: Vector2) -> int: var l_id: int = G_ChunkingServer.register_entity(p_position, 24.0, C_ChunkingServer.TEAM.ATTACKER, 0b1) G_HitServer.register(l_id, M_ModuleManager.new(), build_archer_profile()) return l_id ## The hit side of the archer archetype, shared by every archer. ## @return The profile to register them with func build_archer_profile() -> R_HitProfile: var l_profile: R_HitProfile = R_HitProfile.new() l_profile.yBand = Vector2(0.0, 64.0) l_profile.hurtGroups = CH_PROJECTILE | CH_MELEE l_profile.hitGroups = CH_PROJECTILE l_profile.stopGroups = CH_SHIELD return l_profile ## Fires one arrow: it pierces until a shield stops it. ## @param p_id The archer that shoots ## @param p_origin Where the arrow starts ## @param p_direction Where the arrow flies ## @return The impact positions as (x, y, height) func shoot(p_id: int, p_origin: Vector2, p_direction: Vector2) -> PackedVector3Array: return G_HitServer.hit_directional_rect_ordered( p_id, p_origin, p_direction, 480.0, 18.0, Vector2(20.0, 40.0), C_HitServer.UNLIMITED_HITS, C_HitServer.NO_PREFERRED_TARGET, _arrowHitData, C_HitServer.RECT_EDGE.BACK)

Ordered from BACK, so the arrow walks outward from the archer and the first shield it meets ends it — every entity behind that shield is spared. A shieldbearer's own profile carries hurtGroups = CH_PROJECTILE | CH_MELEE | CH_SHIELD: the projectile bit lets the arrow hit it, the shield bit makes it stop there.

Data

What the hit server stores

Like the chunk index, every per entity field is a packed array indexed by the entity id, grown by s_entitySlotAppended and cleared by s_entityReleased. Positions, radii and teams are not copied — they are read straight from G_ChunkingServer. On top come the shared radius curve storage and a few buffers reused by every hit, so a hit allocates nothing but its result array.

Every row opens for a worked example.

Per entity

FieldTypeHolds
_entityModulesArray[M_ModuleManager]Who receives the hits of each entity

When a hit lands on id 5, the server calls _entityModules[5].hit(p_hitData). null is allowed: the target is still hit — it counts towards the hit limit, can stop the hit, and its impact is returned — but nobody is told. The call happens only after the whole target list is final, so hit() may unregister its own entity safely.

_entityYBandPackedVector2ArrayVertical extent as (bottom, top) — the third axis

Positions are 2D; height lives here. A hit carries its own band, and the two must overlap: hit.bottom ≤ target.top and hit.top ≥ target.bottom.

The overhead strike (70 … 100) misses the archer: 70 ≤ 64 is false. The arrow passes under the bat: 40 ≥ 80 is false. No special case for flying units anywhere.

_entityHitGroups · _entityHurtGroups · _entityStopGroupsPackedInt64ArrayThe three hit channel masks

Explained bit by bit, with the arrow-and-shield example, under Group spaces.

_entityCurveIndexPackedInt32ArrayCurve slot per entity, or NO_CURVE for a constant radius

Which shared radius profile an entity uses (see the storage below). Ids 0 and 1 are goblins, 2 a troll, 3 an archer without a curve:

_entityVisitStampPackedInt32ArrayThe last hit that looked at each entity — dedup in one compare

An entity stands in every chunk its radius touches, so a hit walking chunks 1, 2, 5, 6 meets a large entity up to four times. Each hit first increments _visitStamp, then:

hit #17 walks chunk 1: id 2 stamp 16 ≠ 17 → stamp = 17, test it hit #17 walks chunk 2: id 2 stamp 17 = 17 → skip hit #17 walks chunk 5: id 2 stamp 17 = 17 → skip hit #18 walks chunk 1: id 2 stamp 17 ≠ 18 → stamp = 18, test it

Nothing is ever cleared between hits — a new counter value makes every old stamp stale at once.

Radius curve storage — one slot per archetype, not per entity

How the six curve fields fit together — 50 goblins and 3 trolls

All goblins share one Curve resource, all trolls another. Each distinct curve gets a slot; the slot owns a block of 65 pre-read samples; entities only store the slot number.

## A troll: radius 40, yBand (0, 100), hit measured at height 30 position = (30 - 0) / 100 * 64 = 19.2 # 64 intervals between the 65 samples sample = 19, fraction = 0.2 base = slot 1 * 65 + 19 = 84 percent = s[84] + (s[85] - s[84]) * 0.2 # e.g. 70 + (75 - 70) * 0.2 = 71 radius = 40 * 71 / 100 = 28.4
FieldTypeHolds
_radiiCurvesArray[Curve]The Curve stored in each slot

Never sampled during a hit — it is kept only to find the dictionary key again when the slot is freed. A freed slot holds null.

_curveSamplesPackedFloat32ArrayEvery slot baked into 65 samples, one block per slot

Slot s owns indices s × 65 … s × 65 + 64. Sample i holds the curve's value (0 … 100 %) at i / 64 × 100 % of the entity's height: sample 0 at the feet, 16 at a quarter, 32 halfway, 64 at the top. Read once when a slot is taken; a hit reads two floats and interpolates instead of calling into the Curve object.

_curveSlotOfDictionary[Curve, int]Which slot a curve already sits in
{ goblin.tres: 0, troll.tres: 1 }

This is what makes the 50th goblin free: its curve is found here and it just joins slot 0. Keys are object identity, not curve shape — two separately created Curves with the same points get two slots. Share one .tres per archetype.

_curveUsersPackedInt32ArrayHow many entities use each slot

A reference count. When the third troll dies (or switches curve), slot 1 drops to 0 and is freed: its _radiiCurves entry becomes null, its dictionary key is erased, and 1 goes onto _freeCurveIndices.

_freeCurveIndicesPackedInt32ArraySlots ready to be handed out again

After the trolls are gone: [1]. The next new curve (say, for ogres) takes slot 1 and overwrites samples 65 … 129; no array grows.

Per hit buffers — reused, never shrunk

One ordered hit, buffer by buffer — hit_circle_ordered, limit 3, emitter stops at shields

1 · Gather. The chunk walk finds four opponents that pass team, flags, groups and band. Cells past the count are leftovers from earlier hits and are ignored.

2 · Geometry. Id 9 is out of reach and dropped. The other three are pushed with their key — for a circle, the squared distance to the origin:

3 · Order. _hitOrder lists buffer indices by ascending key — nothing else is moved:

4 · Cut. Limit is min(3, 3) = 3. Walk in order: id 3 (goblin), then id 4 — a shieldbearer, stop groups match, so the walk ends after it. 5 · Dispatch ids 3 and 4, return their two impact positions. Id 7 is spared.

FieldTypeHolds
_visitStampintCounter handed to every gather

Incremented at the start of every chunk gather; entities compare their _entityVisitStamp against it (example above). The preferred target path tests a single id and does not need it.

_candidateIds · _candidateCountPackedInt32Array · intEntities worth a geometry test in the running hit

Only the first _candidateCount entries are live. The count is reset to 0 per hit; the array itself keeps its size and just doubles when full (16 → 32 → 64 …, see BUFFER_MIN_CAPACITY). After a few hits it has reached its working size and never allocates again.

_hitIds · _hitCountPackedInt32Array · intTargets the shape accepted

Same live-up-to-count scheme. Three arrays run in parallel at the same index: _hitIds[i], _hitKeys[i], _hitPositions[i] all describe hit i.

_hitKeysPackedFloat32ArraySort key of each accepted target — smaller goes first

Each shape writes the key that makes "ascending" mean its natural order:

ShapeKeyFirst in line
circledistance² to the originnearest
rect, BACKalongclosest to the back edge
rect, FRONTlength − alongclosest to the front edge
rect, RIGHT / LEFT−across / acrossclosest to that side
cake slice−angle (angle when reversed)at the right (left) edge

Unordered hits still write keys but never sort them.

_hitPositionsPackedVector3ArrayImpact of each accepted target, as (x, y, height)

The point on the target's silhouette facing the hit origin, at the sampled height. A target at (100, 0) with radius 20, hit from the origin (0, 0) at height 30 → (80, 0, 30). If the origin lies inside the target's radius, the impact is the origin itself. These are what the hit functions return — handy for spawning impact effects.

_hitOrderPackedInt32ArrayIndices into the hit buffers, in the order an ordered hit applies them

Built by insertion sort over _hitKeys only for ordered hits that may land more than once. An ordered hit with limit 1 skips it and just picks the smallest key.

_defaultHitProfileR_HitProfileFallback for an entity registered with null

Built once, on first need, with the R_HitProfile defaults: band (0, 64) and every mask 0. That means such an entity can never be hit and its hits connect with nothing — which is why registering without a profile logs an error rather than passing silently.

_hasWarnedPreferredMisuseboolWhether the preferred-target warning was already printed

Passing a preferred target with a limit other than 1 is ignored with a warning. This flag keeps that warning to once per run instead of once per hit.

Shared arguments

ArgumentTypeMeaning
p_emitterIdintThe entity the hit belongs to; its team, hit groups and stop groups drive everything

The shape itself is placed by p_origin, not by the emitter's position — a mage can cast an explosion 300 units away. The emitter only supplies the team (never hit your own), its hit groups and its stop groups.

p_hitYBandVector2Vertical extent of the hit; must overlap a target's band

A ground slam: (0, 10) — misses everything flying. An arrow at chest height: (20, 40). Anti-air flak: (80, 400). Its middle is also the height a radius curve is sampled at. See the band drawing.

p_maxHitsintUpper number of targets, or C_HitServer.UNLIMITED_HITS

In an _ordered hit this means "the first N in order" — with a circle, the nearest N. In a plain hit it means "any N", whichever the chunk walk met first. 0 hits nobody.

p_preferredIdintTried alone first when p_maxHits is 1, else C_HitServer.NO_PREFERRED_TARGET

For a single-target attack that should land on the unit's current target if it can. Pass the id from G_TargetingServer.get_target():

preferred valid and inside the shape → hit it, done — no chunk is walked preferred filtered out or out of reach → normal hit: nearest / any other target p_maxHits != 1 → preference ignored, one warning per run
p_hitDataR_HitDataHanded to every module manager that is hit

One shared object for all targets of this hit. Keep one per attack kind (e.g. an _arrowHitData member) instead of creating one per shot.

returnsPackedVector3ArrayImpacts as (x, y, height), on the target's silhouette facing the origin

One entry per target that was hit, in hit order for the _ordered variants. Empty means nothing was hit. Example for an arrow from (0, 0) hitting a goblin (r 20) at (100, 0) at height 30: [(80, 0, 30)].

C_HitServer

NameKindMeaning
RECT_EDGEenumBACK, FRONT, LEFT, RIGHT — where an ordered rect hit starts

Edges in the rect's own frame, looking along p_direction: BACK is the edge at p_origin, FRONT the far one. A piercing arrow uses BACK (nearest to the shooter first); a wave rolling in from the far end uses FRONT; a sweep from the side uses LEFT or RIGHT. The resulting sort keys are listed under _hitKeys.

UNLIMITED_HITSconst int−1: pass as p_maxHits to hit every valid target

An explosion: UNLIMITED_HITS. A cleave that may hit two: 2. A single-target stab: 1. Stop groups can still end an ordered unlimited hit early.

NO_PREFERRED_TARGETconst int−1: pass as p_preferredId when there is no target to try first

See p_preferredId for what a preferred target does.

NO_CURVEconst int−1: curve slot of an entity with a constant radius

Stored in _entityCurveIndex. Such an entity is a plain cylinder: its full _entityRadius at every height.

CURVE_PERCENT_MAXconst float100: upper end of both curve axes

Author radius curves with x from 0 to 100 (percent of the height, feet to head) and y from 0 to 100 (percent of the radius). A point (70, 100) means "at 70 % of its height, the full radius". A curve left on Godot's default 0 … 1 domain is sampled at 0 … 100, clamps to its last point everywhere, and silently behaves like a cylinder.

BUFFER_MIN_CAPACITYconst int16: first size a per hit buffer grows to

The candidate and hit buffers start empty. The first push grows them to 16, and after that they double when full: 16 → 32 → 64. Without the minimum, the first hits would grow 1 → 2 → 4 → 8 → 16, reallocating four times for nothing.

CURVE_SAMPLE_COUNTconst int65: samples per baked curve

64 equal steps from 0 % to 100 %, plus one so the last sample sits exactly on 100 %. Each step is 1.5625 % of the height.

Costs 65 floats (260 bytes) per distinct curve, whatever the number of entities using it.

Contract

What the caller has to respect

  • Only the other team is ever hit. There is no friendly fire path and no flag to enable one.
  • Announced entities are skipped. An entity marked by pre_unregister_entity() takes no more hits — that is what the flag is for.
  • A preferred target needs a hit limit of exactly 1. Anything else logs a warning and runs as a normal hit.
  • Radius curves run 0 to 100 on both axes. A curve on the default domain behaves like no curve at all. Curves are shared by resource identity, so give an archetype one .tres.
  • Always pass a profile. Without one the entity gets all-zero masks: it can never be hit and its hits connect with nothing.
  • Hit, hurt and stop groups are one 64 bit space, separate from the chunking groups. Define the channel bits once and use them in all three masks; do not reuse the group bits of the chunk masks here.
  • Entities call the hit server directly. It is an autoload loaded after G_ChunkingServer and has no id space of its own.
  • A module may kill its own entity inside hit(). That is safe, because dispatch happens after the target list is closed.

Verification

The resolution logic was fuzz-tested against a brute-force reference: 40 worlds × 120 hits across all three shapes, comparing the chunk-narrowed result with a scan of every entity alive. No misses, no duplicates, and team, flag, group and band filters correct in every case; hit limits, sort orders and stop-group cuts were checked separately on top.

That validated the algorithm, ahead of the GDScript. The end-to-end suite in res://Tests/EndToEndTest.gd now exercises hits inside Godot itself, through G_ChunkingServer and G_TargetingServer together.
