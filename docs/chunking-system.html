Overview

One grid, two kinds of membership, three levels of summary

The map is cut into square chunks. A vertical stack of chunks is a column; all columns together are the map. Columns are the middle level on purpose: the two bases face each other along x, so a column is a slice of the front line, and "did an opponent get behind me?" becomes a question about columns.

G_ChunkingServer itself is small: it has two setters, set_position() and set_radius(). All the data and the code that keeps it correct live on A_CoreServer, because registration and removal need them as well. This page explains how that index works; the core page explains every one of its variables with an example and a drawing.

C_ChunkingServer

Square chunks, whatever the configuration says

You configure a map size and a wanted number of columns and rows. _static_init() then makes every chunk square: it averages the two edge lengths the configuration implies, and uses as many chunks of that edge as it takes to cover the map on each axis. The grid can therefore end up slightly larger than configured, never smaller. With the shipped configuration (4096 × 4096 in 32 × 32) nothing changes: 128 × 128 chunks, 32 × 32 of them.

# a configuration that does not fit: 5000 × 3000 in 40 columns × 20 rows column edge = 5000 / 40 = 125 row edge = 3000 / 20 = 150 CHUNK_SIZE = (125 + 150) / 2 = 137.5 CHUNK_COLUMNS = ceil(5000 / 137.5 − 0.0001) = ceil(36.36) = 37 CHUNK_ROWS = ceil(3000 / 137.5 − 0.0001) = ceil(21.82) = 22 MAP_SIZE = (37, 22) * 137.5 = (5087.5, 3025)
Membership

Slots for hits, centers for searches

Slots. An entity stands in every chunk its bounding square position ± radius touches. Each such fact is one membership slot, linked into two chains: the chain of its chunk and the chain of its entity. A hit walks the slot chains of the chunks under its shape, so it finds large entities even when only their edge reaches in. A visit stamp keeps it from collecting the same entity twice.

Centers. Every entity is also in exactly one center chain: the one of the chunk its position falls into. A targeting search walks center chains ring by ring, so every candidate is scored exactly once, and its chunk distance is measured from one well defined chunk.

Every variable named here is explained with a drawing on the core page: slot chains, center chains, counts, masks.

Moving

Only the difference is touched

set_position(id, position) writes the position, computes the new chunk area and hands it to _move_to_chunk_area(), then calls _update_center_chunk(). Both steps return at once when nothing changed, which is by far the common case: an entity walking a few units per tick almost never crosses a chunk border. set_radius() does the same with the radius, and skips the center step, because the center does not move with the radius.

Worked example: id 2 moves from (256, 128) to (300, 250)

new square = (260, 210) … (340, 290) → columns 2 … 2, rows 1 … 2 → area (2, 1, 2, 2) old area = (1, 0, 2, 1) → chunks 1, 2, 5, 6 walk id 2's slot chain 5 → 4 → 3 → 2: slot 5 (chunk 6) inside new area → keep slot 4 (chunk 5) outside → _remove_membership(4) slot 3 (chunk 2) outside → _remove_membership(3) slot 2 (chunk 1) outside → _remove_membership(2) walk the new area, add chunks outside the old one: chunk 6 (row 1) inside old → skip; chunk 10 (row 2) → _add_membership(2, 10) center: floor(300 / 128), floor(250 / 128) = (2, 1) → chunk 6, unchanged → no center work
Summaries

Counts are written through, masks cascade only while they change

Per team and level there are two summaries: a membership count (chunk and column) and a group mask (chunk, column, map). Counts are simple: every add and remove writes ±1 into its chunk and its column. Masks are asymmetric: adding is mask |= groups, but removing cannot just clear the entity's bits, because another entity of the same team may still carry them. So a removal rebuilds the mask from the level below. Two rules keep that cheap:

Stop upwards

A level is only touched when the level below changed. Adding a GROUND unit where GROUND already is ends after the chunk.

Stop early

A rebuild can only shrink the mask. Its running OR is compared to the old value after every step; once they match, the rest cannot change it, so it returns "unchanged" immediately.

Column span

Leftmost and rightmost column per team, kept for free

_teamMinColumn and _teamMaxColumn are updated inside _change_membership_counts(), only when a column count crosses 0. A column getting its first membership widens the span if it lies outside. A column losing its last membership only matters if it is an end of the span: then the span scans inwards to the next non-empty column; if the span was that one column, the team is gone (both ends NO_ID). Targeting reads the span to answer "did an opponent get behind me?" in two comparisons per team.

Built on the index

How hit and targeting read it

Hit: counts and slot chains

A hit computes the chunk area under its shape's bounds, then per column asks "does any other team count here?", per chunk the same, and only then walks the chunk's slot chain. A visit stamp per entity dedups entities standing in several chunks. More on the hit page.

Targeting: masks and center chains

A search first asks the map mask "does any opponent have a searched group?", then per ring column the column mask, per chunk the chunk mask, and only then walks the chunk's center chain. More on the targeting page.

Contract

What the caller has to respect

  • Move and resize only through set_position() and set_radius(). Writing _entityPosition or _entityRadius directly leaves the chains, counts and masks describing the old state.
  • Positions outside the map are clamped into the border chunks. They do not error, but everything beyond the edge piles into the edge chunks.
  • A radius much larger than a chunk costs one slot per covered chunk and a longer walk on every move that crosses a border.
  • The grid is fixed on class load. Change the constants and restart to change it.
